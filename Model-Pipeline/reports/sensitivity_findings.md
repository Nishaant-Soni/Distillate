Written by hand from the numbers below (run of 2026-11-13 on the selected student, 21,363 validation rows, 3 h 20 min on CPU). Everything is on validation, so these are diagnostics, not final numbers.

1. **The student's quality hardly depends on α and T inside the region it was tuned in.** Maximum length explains roughly 35 to 55% of the study's variance and ranks first under every seed (over 11 seeds, fANOVA 0.37 to 0.55, MDI 0.33 to 0.48), then epochs (0.24 to 0.34 / 0.22 to 0.32); α (0.10 to 0.23 / 0.12 to 0.22), learning rate and T explain the rest. These are shares of a small variance: the 28 trials span validation mean AUC 0.988 to 0.9936. Maximum length leads because every 128-token trial scores below every good 256-token trial, and epochs mostly because of the 1-epoch trials. TPE sampled mostly near the best region, so α and T far from it are under-explored.

2. **Obfuscation is the student's clearest weakness, and it fails quietly.** Replacing characters with look-alikes (a→4, e→3, …) at rate 0.1, which changes 4.3% of characters, already makes it miss 14.5% of the toxic comments it caught when clean (mean over labels; toxicity 9.1%, threat 31%, 14 of the 45 threats caught when clean). At rate 0.5 (21% of characters) it misses 54% (toxicity 38%, threat 98% (44 of 45), identity_attack 72% (86 of 120)), and mean AUC falls from 0.994 to 0.942. The flag rate falls with it (3.95% of comments clean, 2.44% at 0.5), so obfuscated text is passed as clean rather than flagged: attacks lower recall without raising false alarms. Threat, insult and identity_attack depend most on exact words; obscene is missed least (27% at 0.5). The fixed obfuscation set (toxic gate comments, several tricks at once) points the same way: toxicity recall 0.880 on the originals, 0.655 obfuscated (`reports/student/phase2/results.md`).

3. **Identity words push the student toward flagging, which matches the bias report.** In SHAP's false alarms for identity_attack, the most-attributed words are a racial slur (+7.3; slurs are shown with their inner letters masked), "gay" (+5.2), "nazi" (+3.9) and "racist" (+2.8); "gay" also pushes caught toxicity comments up (+2.6). Comments that mention or discuss an identity, including ones that quote or report abuse, are scored as attacks. This is the mechanism behind the bias report's finding that the soft-label students flag more non-toxic comments mentioning race than the gold-only student does (section 9 of the model card). Threat false alarms work the same way: "kill" (+6.5) and "die" (+5.1) outside a threat.

4. **Missed toxic comments carry little attribution.** For toxicity the missed group's top words have small attributions (none above +0.7). The missed toxicity, insult, threat and identity_attack examples are mostly insults without profanity, threats without "kill" or "die", toxicity carried by context, and identity attacks without a slur; the missed severe_toxicity and obscene examples do contain profanity, which the student scored as toxic but not severe or obscene enough. The student relies on explicit words; implicit toxicity is its main source of false negatives on clean text.

5. **Truncating to 256 tokens costs little; 64 would cost the long comments.** At 256 tokens 6.4% of validation is truncated, and those comments score mean AUC 0.988 against 0.993 for the rest. Cutting to 64 tokens truncates 41% and lowers mean AUC by 0.004 overall (0.990), but by 0.023 on comments of 129 to 256 tokens (0.993 to 0.970); comments of 64 tokens or fewer are unaffected (0.993). A faster 64- or 128-token student would mainly lose long comments, a cost to weigh against latency in Phase 3.

Limits: SHAP is per label on 12 comments per outcome (211 comments in all), so the word tables show what drives these comments, not frequencies; a word must occur in at least 5 validation comments to be listed, and the masked examples show no two adjacent words and never a comment's first or last word. The soft-label arms' validation numbers may be slightly optimistic (model card section 3, validation caveat), which affects levels here but not the comparisons across lengths and rates.
